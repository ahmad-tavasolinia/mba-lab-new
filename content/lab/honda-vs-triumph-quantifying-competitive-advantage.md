For decades, British motorcycle manufacturers such as Triumph competed in a market shaped by established traditions and production practices. Honda challenged this position with a more cost-efficient operation and a product that offered customers greater value. As Honda strengthened its position, NVT struggled to retain market share, with its sales declining rapidly.

This project uses the competitive advantage framework from [Wharton’s Business Strategy course](/courses/wharton-business-strategy-competitive-advantage) to quantify the difference between Honda and Triumph/NVT. By examining production costs and estimating customers’ willingness to pay, it shows how Honda’s cost advantage of approximately $460 and estimated willingness-to-pay advantage of $250 combine to create a total competitive advantage of roughly $710 per motorcycle.

The analysis demonstrates why competitive advantage is about more than charging a lower price or producing at a lower cost. It depends on the difference between the value customers place on a product and the cost of delivering it, relative to competitors.
